**Blurt — Campfire Word Game**
Last updated: 18 August 2026

## The short version

Blurt does not collect anything. There are no accounts, no analytics, no
tracking, no advertising, and no servers. Nothing you do in the app leaves your
device.

## What Blurt stores

Blurt saves the settings you choose, so the app opens the way you left it:

- The mode, category and timer length you last picked
- Whether the Kids switch is on

That is all. Blurt does not record your scores between rounds, and it does not
keep a history of the words you have seen. The settings are written to the
app's private storage on your device and are not readable by other apps.

## Where it goes

Nowhere. Blurt contains no networking code — it never contacts a server,
because there is no server to contact. The words themselves ship inside the
app, so it works with the phone in airplane mode.

If you have iCloud or iTunes device backups turned on, iOS may include Blurt's
settings in your own encrypted backup, as it does for other apps. That backup
is between you and Apple; Blurt is not involved and cannot read it.

## What Blurt does not do

- No analytics or crash reporting SDKs
- No advertising identifiers and no ad networks
- No tracking across apps or websites
- No account, sign-in, or email address
- No location, contacts, photos, camera, or microphone access
- No third-party SDKs of any kind
- No purchases inside the app

## Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything Blurt has stored. There is no copy anywhere
else, so there is nothing else to delete and no request to make.

## Children

Blurt is a party game meant to be played by a group, including children. It
collects no personal information from anyone, of any age. The Kids mode changes
which words appear; it does not change how the app handles data, because the
app handles none.
